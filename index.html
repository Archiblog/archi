01

The archival trace is present, but the object is not.

I’ve had a fairly thorough look, and I can’t find any convincing trace of a 1995 project by Cedric Price specifically called Archi.

What does appear in the archival record around 1994 to 1996 is a cluster of late Price projects with deliberately abbreviated or coded names: Magnet, Task Force, AFX, Gatard, Walsall, Halmag, South Bank II, and others. The main listing is in the Canadian Centre for Architecture’s Cedric Price fonds archive, the authoritative catalogue of his office records.

02

The blank is not a failure of research. It may be the finding.

I think I may have identified something quite important: not simply a missing project, but a productive absence.

There is effectively no public architectural discourse around Archi.

The normal secondary literature on Cedric Price does not mention it.

Even deep searches through theses, archival references, and late-work discussions turn up nothing identifiable beyond the observation of Project 223.

Most Price projects, even unrealised or fragmentary ones, leave at least a residue: a lecture mention, a site, collaborators, a drawing, or an anecdotal afterlife. Archi appears almost as a catalogue placeholder without a corresponding public object.

03

This blankness aligns with Price’s late thinking.

  • Architecture as anticipation rather than solution
  • Architecture as enabling condition
  • Architecture as time-based process
  • Architecture as informational or organisational framework

04 / Correspondence

The enquiry now becomes part of the evidence.

The research has moved from a bibliographic absence into a direct archival question. In writing to the Canadian Centre for Architecture, the project is no longer only being inferred from catalogue numbering; it is being tested against the institution that holds the Cedric Price fonds.

Enquiry to the CCA Archives Team

From Norman Fellows

Dear Archives Team,

I am writing with a research enquiry concerning Cedric Price Project No. 223, titled “Archi”, which is listed in Samantha Hardingham’s cataloguing of Cedric Price’s work.

While consulting the Cedric Price fonds at the Canadian Centre for Architecture, I have been unable to identify any corresponding material for this project within Series 2 - Projects, nor elsewhere in the currently accessible finding aids. I wondered whether the CCA might hold any uncatalogued, provisional, administrative, or cross-referenced material associated with Project 223 “Archi”, or whether the project is known only through office numbering references.

My interest in this enquiry arises from ongoing independent research into Cedric Price’s later work and its relation to open-ended and anticipatory architectural frameworks. I interviewed Cedric Price in July 1996 during earlier stages of this research, and I am currently revisiting aspects of that work in connection with a broader study concerning architecture as a progressing rather than fixed condition.

I fully appreciate that there may ultimately be little or no surviving material associated with the project. Even clarification regarding the absence of records would nevertheless be extremely valuable for the purposes of my research.

If there are any relevant references, internal notes, related project files, or suggestions for further avenues of enquiry, I would be very grateful.

With thanks for your time and assistance.

Kind regards,
Norman Fellows

CCA reply

From Loris Kecaj

Dear Norman,

Thank you for your interest in our Collection.

After consulting the Cedric Price fonds finding aid, I was not able to identify any records related to the project “No. 223” titled “Archi”. I also consulted Early Work and Miscellaneous Records, but did not find anything.

In Samantha Hardingham’s cataloguing of Price’s work, does she provide any information on the description of the “Archi” project?

Perhaps, there may be information in the sub-series, Filing System which contains textual records related to Cedric Price's filing system including job lists and reference cards (which includes material from 1966-1996). I would be happy to take a look at these documents on your behalf. Although the soonest I would be able to do this would be in the Fall (sometime in September or October). This is because from June 9 to the end of August, the CCA hosts a number of scholars-in-residence. These researchers will be given priority in reserving appointments to consult CCA material in the study room and, unfortunately, we are unable to accommodate external requests to access archival materials during this period.

Please let me know how you would like to proceed and if you have any questions.

Best regards,
Loris Kecaj

Follow-up to CCA

From Norman Fellows

Dear Loris,

Many thanks for your helpful reply and for taking the time to investigate this enquiry.

I was particularly interested by your mention of the Filing System sub-series and the associated job lists and reference cards, as this may indeed be the most likely location for any trace of the project.

Further to your question regarding Samantha Hardingham’s cataloguing, I attach a scan of the relevant page in which Project No. 223 “Archi” is listed with the date 1995. Unfortunately, no further description appears to be provided there beyond the project number, title, and year.

For reference, the source is:

Samantha Hardingham (ed.), Cedric Price Works 1952-2003: A Forward-Minded Retrospective, London: Architectural Association, 2003.

Please do not feel under any pressure regarding timescales - I completely understand the current access limitations and appreciate your offer to look into the Filing System material later in the year.

With many thanks again for your assistance.

Kind regards,
Norman Fellows

What this changes

The correspondence gives the absence a sharper contour. Archi is confirmed in Hardingham’s catalogue as Project No. 223, dated 1995, but with no description beyond number, title, and year. It is not visible in the Cedric Price fonds finding aid, Series 2 - Projects, Early Work, or Miscellaneous Records.

The remaining lead is administrative rather than project-based: the Filing System sub-series, including job lists and reference cards from 1966 to 1996. If Archi appears there, it may survive as an office-numbering or filing event before it survives as an architectural project.

05

The title itself refuses completion.

Archi feels unfinished: a prefix, a stem, the beginning of architecture, but not the completed noun. Almost a linguistic equivalent of an open system.

That may be accidental, but with Price one hesitates to dismiss linguistic compression or irony too quickly.

06

Use the gap operationally.

In the CCA archive numbering system, project numbers are sometimes assigned before a project coheres publicly. Project 223 may represent an enquiry, a proposal framework, a provisional research thread, or simply an office holding pattern that never stabilised into a conventional architectural commission.

That may be precisely its value. Not as a lost project to reconstruct historically, but as a deliberately underdetermined field from which to continue the project of redefining architecture.

Archi may be useful because it resists closure.

07

A hinge for architecture as progression.

Rather than treating the absence as a research failure, it can be positioned as a terminal gap in Price’s catalogue, an unrealised anticipatory condition, or an architectural open variable.

The fact that Archi has almost no sedimented interpretation may make it unusually available for reactivation. Present enough to receive a project number but absent from the usual discourse, it occupies a peculiar threshold condition in the oeuvre.

  • Architecture before formal resolution
  • Architecture as ongoing disposition
  • Architecture as anticipatory field
  • Architecture as recursive continuation

What is especially interesting here is that this does not try to complete the missing project historically. It treats the gap as operational, almost as a live site for theoretical continuation. That feels aligned with Price’s resistance to closure and architectural finality.
